# External review, 2026-07-30: verification and adopted plan

An external source-level review recommended stopping heuristic work for a
cycle and fixing the pipeline's correctness boundary. Before adopting it, its
checkable claims were verified against this codebase and against rawler
0.7.2's actual source. This document records what was confirmed, what was
overstated, what is adopted in which order, and the verdict on the `zenraw/`
checkout as a shortcut.

## Claims verified true (checked in source, 2026-07-30)

1. **Rawler's `Calibrate` destroys information before we see it.** Worse than
   the review stated: `imgop/raw.rs` `clip_euclidean_norm_avg` clips negative
   (out-of-gamut) channels *unconditionally*, and replaces any pixel with a
   channel above 1.0 — which, after white-balance gains, is a large share of
   highlights — with an average of its normalized colour and its Euclidean
   norm. The conversion is a hardcoded sRGB-D65 normalized pseudo-inverse; no
   `ForwardMatrix`, no `CameraCalibration`, no dual-illuminant interpolation.
   Everything this program's analysis and rendering does happens downstream of
   that loss. This is also why 0.2's highlight-reconstruction item cannot work
   as planned today: the clipped-channel information is gone before demosaic
   output reaches us.
2. **`whitebalance.rs`'s luminance invariant is false.** The module doc argues
   that unit-luminance white points make the correction exposure-neutral, but
   the correction *divides* by the white point per channel, and the reciprocal
   of a unit-luminance vector does not have unit luminance. The feature is
   opt-in and off, so nothing shipped is wrong, but the documented invariant
   does not hold and needs a luminance-restore step plus a test over
   non-neutral colours.
3. **Sharpening runs on sRGB-encoded u16 output.** True as a fact. (The
   review's hue argument is overstated — an equal additive offset preserves
   hue angle and channel ordering; what it does is desaturate slightly toward
   white — but "equal code-value steps are unequal linear-light steps" is
   correct, and the fixed 1 px radius does mean different things at different
   output sizes.)
4. **`noise.rs` materializes the full RAW a second time** (`collect_tiles`
   maps the whole sample array into a `Vec<f32>`). ~200 MB avoidable on 50 MP.
5. **Output carries no ICC/EXIF** — already `docs/PLAN.md` 0.2 item 1; the
   review is right that untagged 16-bit TIFF also weakens visual comparisons.
6. **`levels.rs` CFA normalization is a documented punt** waiting on owning
   normalization.

## Claims adopted with modification

- **Separate independent-Auto from preview-guided evaluation.** `docs/PLAN.md`
  §4b already states the borrow-the-judgement position; what is genuinely
  missing is mechanical: a `guidance_mode` field in the sidecar, a
  `--no-preview` (independent) scorecard column in the survey tooling, and
  renaming the `subject` statistic to what it is (`center_weighted_key_ev`).
  Cheap, adopted.
- **Freeze-and-refit controller (v1/v2).** The freeze discipline is adopted as
  policy: no new exposure heuristics until the colour core lands. The *fitted*
  v2 controller is *deferred*: the review's own requirement — split train/test
  by scene, camera, lighting — collapses the current corpus (106 pairs, one
  photographer, essentially three shooting locations) to nothing. Gathering
  the graded corpus first was already the plan (§6). Revisit after the corpus
  has scene diversity.
- **Memory milestone.** Real, but `--local-tone` is off by default and
  measured out, so its 2 GB ceiling blocks nothing shipping. Adopted piecemeal:
  two-plane chroma representation next time `chroma.rs` is touched; the noise
  clone fix is trivial and can ride along; `--jobs`-from-RAM is already
  planned. Full tiling and `--memory-limit` wait until the colour core exists,
  because buffer layout changes with it.
- **Comparison-harness alignment.** Matters when crops/dimensions differ
  between ours and the camera JPEG. For the current corpus (native-resolution
  Sony pairs) gradient comparison is same-geometry; Samsung pairs differ and
  should be excluded from gradient rows until canonical resizing exists.
  Adopted as a caveat now, tooling later.

## Claims rejected or deferred, with reasons

- **RCD/AMaZE demosaic.** ~~The quality implementations live in GPL-3 codebases
  (RawTherapee, darktable); this crate is MIT. Porting is a licence decision,
  not an engineering one~~ — **the licence objection was removed in 0.1.18**,
  when the project relicensed from MIT to AGPL-3.0-or-later precisely so that
  GPL-family RAW tooling stops being off limits. What remains of the original
  reasoning still stands on its own: PPG is not the current quality bottleneck on
  the A7C/Samsung target sources, and X-Trans is explicitly out of scope. So this
  is now deferred on merit and sequencing rather than refused, and it is a real
  candidate once the colour core is finished. The `Demosaicer` trait shape is
  noted for when the raw core exists.
- **Comments→experiment-files migration.** The evidence-in-comments style is
  this project's deliberate record; CHANGELOG.md already is the experiment
  log. Not adopted wholesale; long narratives may move to `docs/` case by case.

## zenraw verdict (surveyed 2026-07-30)

`zenraw/` is imazen's zenraw v0.2.0 — a RAW/DNG *decoder* front-end, not a
developer, licensed **AGPL-3.0-only OR commercial**. The licence objection
recorded here — "this crate is MIT: depending on it AGPLs the product" — **no
longer applies as of 0.1.18**, since the project is now AGPL-3.0-or-later itself.
The verdict does not change, because it never rested on the licence: on the merits
zenraw would not help. It has no highlight reconstruction, no hot-pixel handling,
its demosaic is bilinear/Malvar (≈ rawler's tier), and it pins the *same* rawler
0.7.2 optionally as a backend. The one instructive file is `src/dng_render.rs`
(Bradford adaptation, AsShotNeutral→xy, matrix composition — though its
ForwardMatrix support is parsed-but-unused, CameraCalibration is absent, and
dual-illuminant interpolation is dead code). **Use it as reading material for
sequencing the DNG colour math and implement from the Adobe DNG spec.** The
checkout was removed from the tree after the survey; nothing was taken from it.

## Adopted order (next release = the correctness boundary)

1. **`--raw-color-path {rawler|owned}` A/B switch.** Stop Rawler after
   `Demosaic` (camera RGB out), implement as-shot WB + camera→XYZ→working
   conversion ourselves — negatives preserved, values above 1 preserved, no
   gamut mapping, no highlight clip. First milestone replicates rawler's
   matrix result minus the clipping, so the diff isolates exactly what
   `clip_euclidean_norm_avg` was costing; the pairs and the
   saturated/clipped corpus classes are the test set. `rawler` stays the
   default until the owned path beats it on the scorecard.
2. **Typed colour-space images** (`Image<CameraRgb>` / `Image<SceneLinear>` /
   `Image<DisplayLinear>` / encoded) as part of that plumbing, so stage order
   is compiler-checked rather than commented.
3. **Sidecar `guidance_mode` + independent-Auto scorecard + `subject` rename**
   (schema bump).
4. **Local-WB luminance restore + non-neutral-colour test.**
5. **ICC embed + EXIF copy** (existing 0.2 item 1, unchanged).
6. **Re-baseline the paired scorecard** before touching any tuned constant:
   the 1.20 saturation multiplier and parts of the highlight handling may be
   compensations for the narrow clipped path and may shrink or vanish.

Full DNG colour science (`ForwardMatrix`, `CameraCalibration`,
dual-illuminant, CAT) is milestone 2 of the colour path, after the unclipped
A/B proves out. Sharpening's move to display-linear is re-evaluated *after*
the colour core, against the pairs, not before.

## Outcome, shipped as 0.1.17

All six adopted items landed. `CHANGELOG.md` 0.1.17 has the full measurements;
this section records only where the *review itself* turned out to be wrong, so
that the next review of this file is reading a corrected document.

**The A/B verdict: the owned path does not yet beat rawler, so rawler stays the
default.** That was this document's own gate and it held. What the A/B bought is
a diagnosis, not a win.

Four corrections to the claims above:

1. **Claim 1 had the emphasis backwards.** It leads on highlights above 1.0. By
   pixel count the dominant loss is the *other* half: median `negative_fraction`
   0.028% against `above_one_fraction` 0.009%, and on the worst corpus frame
   61.1% negative against 5.5% above 1.0. Out-of-gamut colour clipped to zero was
   the bigger cost, not blown highlights.
2. **`clip_euclidean_norm_avg` is not a clip and does not bound its output.**
   `[2.3, 1.0, 1.6]` becomes `[1.359, 1.076, 1.207]`. It destroys the channel
   *ratio* — spread 1.30 → 0.28 — while the magnitude mostly survives. "Squashed
   highlights" was the wrong picture; "desaturated highlights" is right.
3. **The cost is far more skewed than "a large share of highlights" implies.**
   Median 0.215% of pixels rewritten, but 38 of 376 frames above 10% and 18 above
   25%. Most frames barely cared; a minority were wrecked.
4. **Item 6's suspicion about the tuned constants is not borne out.** The 1.20
   saturation multiplier was *not* a compensation for the narrow clipped path:
   the saturation ratio against the camera moves 1.046 → 1.050 → 1.052 across
   rawler, owned/srgb and owned/rec2020. Nothing needed retuning. Exposure
   decisions moved by a median of 0.0000 EV over 376 files.

One thing the review did not anticipate at all, found while owning the matrix
selection: **rawler's `Calibrate` picks its calibration matrix
nondeterministically** when a file carries several and none is D65
(`find(D65).or_else(|| color_matrix.iter().next())` over a `HashMap`). No corpus
file is affected — all 376 carry a D65 matrix — but it is a latent determinism
break in the shipped default path, and the summary now counts and warns.

**The next piece of colour work is therefore not more colour science.** The owned
path has one real regression — crushed shadows on 21 frames, which the entropy row
reports a second time (`r = -0.81`) rather than independently.

The cause is negative **luminance**, not negative channels, and the distinction
decides the fix. `analyze::luminance` is a signed sum;
`tone::render_pixel_local` clamps its chroma anchor with
`luminance(rgb).clamp(0.0, 1.0)`, so a negative-luminance pixel anchors at 0, and
`compress_gamut`'s scale becomes `0 / |min|` = 0 — zeroing every channel including
the positive ones. Rawler's per-channel clip keeps those positive channels, so on
this cohort **the owned path is strictly more destructive than the clip it
replaced**. `clip_cost.negative_luminance_fraction` predicts the regression exactly
(0 false positives, 0 false negatives over 108 frames).

So the fix is a **policy for luminance ≤ 0 pixels**, not gamut compression as such
— and specifically not "preserve hue and luminance", which is impossible there:
every non-negative RGB triple has non-negative luminance. Order: floor
`compress_gamut`'s anchor at `black_output_linear`; split negatives by magnitude
against the fitted noise floor, since the largest negative populations sit on the
noisiest frames in the corpus (`snr10_ev` +1.7 to +2.0 EV) and are noise rather
than scene colour; then a scene-linear operator with the degenerate branch
explicit. This still outranks
`ForwardMatrix`/`CameraCalibration`/dual-illuminant for mechanism — the matrix
changes which colours land out of gamut, not what happens to them — but fitted
thresholds are matrix-dependent, so tune after the colour science, not before.

Two limits on what this A/B could have shown, worth recording before the next one
is designed. The `crushed_fraction` row is **structurally** unable to favour the
owned path: post-`Calibrate` data has no negatives, so rawler cannot crush by this
route while the owned path can only add zeros. And the owned path's actual benefit
— out-of-gamut hue fidelity instead of a silent hue shift — is invisible to all
four axes. Correction 4 above is likewise weaker than stated: `mean_saturation`
averages the whole frame, so it is nearly blind to a desaturation confined to the
0.009%-of-frame above-one cohort. "The global constant needs no change" holds;
"1.20 was not compensating in highlights" is not yet measured.

Two milestone-1 items from the review's own source are still not adopted, and
this document's reasoning above stands: **FujiRotate** (X-Trans out of scope;
rawler 0.7.2 has no such step) and **sharpening before sRGB encoding** (deferred
to after the colour core, measured against the pairs).
